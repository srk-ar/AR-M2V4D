The five formulations are evaluated individually and combined into the three sets that the tables and figures identify as `IDn`: `rigid`, `flexible` and `ensemble`. The displacement model provides individual realizations for AM88, JB07, SR08, BT07, and a fifth formulation selected by the tectonic context: BM17 for projects with subduction control and BM19 for projects with shallow crustal control. The overall design set, identified as `ensemble`, combines AM88, JB07, SR08, BT07, and the active BM branch with equal weights; the rigid family combines AM88, JB07, and SR08, and the flexible family combines BT07 and the active BM branch. The rigid family thus relates the displacement to measures of the base excitation, the flexible family additionally incorporates the dynamic response of the sliding mass through $T_s$, and the ensemble is the design set that combines both. For each $k_y$, the displacements of a family are summarized from the weighted empirical distribution of the realizations of its models.

All models receive the same joint sample of PGA and spectral accelerations, and a single realization of $\epsilon$ is shared across the models and across all values of $k_y$; each formulation scales that residual through its own $\sigma_{\ln D}$, and the sampled PGA is retained with each realization so that the inversion preserves the pairing between displacement and seismic demand. The realizations of $D_n$ are calculated for each material class with its mean dynamic properties; the fractiles of the stiffness profiles and of $T_s$ are reported in Chapter [-@sec-slope_periods] and are not propagated as additional realizations in the displacement calculation, and each material class is kept as a separate scenario through the inversion to $k_{\max}$, so the displacement curves are not averaged across materials before that operation.

For each model and realization, the computed $D_n (k_y)$ relation is projected onto a non-increasing curve and inverted in the $(\ln D_n,\ln k_y)$ plane to solve the criterion of [-@eq-kmax-criterion]; when $D_a$ falls outside the calculated range, the relation is extrapolated in that plane from the two points at the corresponding end, with the single exception of the AM88 limit treated in Appendix [-@sec-newmark-appendix]. The inversion is performed first for each model within each realization. The root distributions of the models are then combined with the family weights and summarized through the arithmetic mean and the empirical fractiles $q=0.05$, 0.10, 0.16, 0.50, 0.84, 0.90, and 0.95 [@VerriKozlowski2026Newmark]. Because of the decreasing relation between $D_n$ and $k_y$, the fractile associated with the exceedance target satisfies expression [-@eq-kmax-fractile], where $q=0.84$ corresponds to an exceedance probability of $p_e=16\%$.

$$
q=1-p_e
$${#eq-kmax-fractile}

The $q$ fractiles are therefore the statistics associated with the criterion expressed through $p_e$; the arithmetic mean summarizes the center of the simulated distribution and does not represent a target probability. The evaluation uses `r .fmti(NS)` realizations for each demand -- the probabilistic spectrum of each return period $T_R$ = `r paste(prettyNum(round(sort(TR.gmdp), 0), big.mark = ","), collapse = ", ")` years and the MCE -- and a grid of allowable displacements $D_a$ ranging from `r paste(format(range(Da.gmdp), trim = TRUE, scientific = FALSE), collapse = " to ")` cm. This grid contains, for every slope height, the damage thresholds $D_a/H$ = `r paste(.fmt(DAMAGE.CUTS, 1), collapse = ", ")`% of Chapter [-@sec-criteria], at which the coefficient tables are reported and which the coefficient figures shade; values of $D_a$ that exceed the calculated range of a realization are obtained through the logarithmic extrapolation defined above.
