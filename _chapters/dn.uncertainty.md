The five formulations are evaluated individually and combined into the three sets that the tables and figures identify as `IDn`: `rigid`, `flexible` and `ensemble`. The displacement model provides individual realizations for AM88, JB07, SR08, BT07, and a fifth formulation selected by the tectonic context: BM17 for projects with subduction control and BM19 for projects with shallow crustal control. The overall design set, identified as `ensemble`, combines AM88, JB07, SR08, BT07, and the active BM branch with equal weights; the rigid family combines AM88, JB07, and SR08, and the flexible family combines BT07 and the active BM branch. The rigid family thus relates the displacement to measures of the base excitation, the flexible family additionally incorporates the dynamic response of the sliding mass through $T_s$, and the ensemble is the design set that combines both. For each $k_y$, the displacements of a family are summarized from the weighted empirical distribution of the realizations of its models. Each formulation enters with the unit weight of its switch in the project contract, and a formulation switched off leaves the three sets.

All models receive the same joint sample of PGA and spectral accelerations, and a single realization of $\epsilon$ is shared across the models and across all values of $k_y$; each formulation scales that residual through its own $\sigma_{\ln D}$, and the sampled PGA is retained with each realization so that the inversion preserves the pairing between displacement and seismic demand. The realizations of $D_n$ are calculated for each material class with its mean dynamic properties; the fractiles of the stiffness profiles and of $T_s$ are reported in Chapter [-@sec-slope_periods] and are not propagated as additional realizations in the displacement calculation, and each material class is kept as a separate scenario through the inversion to $k_{\max}$, so the displacement curves are not averaged across materials before that operation.

For each model and realization, the computed $D_n (k_y)$ relation, with the displacements below $10^{-4}$ cm set to zero, is projected onto its upper non-increasing envelope and inverted in the $(\ln D_n,\ln k_y)$ plane to solve the criterion of [-@eq-kmax-criterion]. When $D_a$ exceeds the largest displacement of the envelope, the root is $k_{\max}=0$; when $D_a$ is smaller than the smallest positive displacement of an envelope that reaches the zero floor, the root is the first $k_y$ of that floor; when $D_a$ is smaller than the displacement at the largest $k_y$ of a curve that stays positive over the whole grid, the root is continued beyond the grid with the closed form of the model, whose free constants are recovered from its points of largest $k_y$, and for AM88 and SR08 it is sought within $k_y/\mathrm{PGA}\leq0.9999$, with the sampled PGA of the realization, $0.9999\,\mathrm{PGA}$ being assigned when the curve at that limit still exceeds $D_a$; the AM88 limit is treated in Appendix [-@sec-newmark-appendix]. The products give, for each cell and each $D_a$, the weighted share of the pooled roots obtained beyond the grid. The inversion is performed first for each model within each realization. The root distributions of the models are then combined with the family weights, the roots $k_{\max}=0$ included with their weight, and summarized through the arithmetic mean and the empirical fractiles $q=0.05$, 0.10, 0.16, 0.50, 0.84, 0.90, and 0.95 [@VerriKozlowski2026Newmark]. Because of the decreasing relation between $D_n$ and $k_y$, the fractile associated with the exceedance target satisfies expression [-@eq-kmax-fractile], where $q=0.84$ corresponds to an exceedance probability of $p_e=16\%$.

$$
q=1-p_e
$${#eq-kmax-fractile}

The $q$ fractiles are therefore the statistics associated with the criterion expressed through $p_e$, each of them an interpolated estimator over the pooled roots, so that the weighted fraction of roots larger than it can exceed $p_e$; the arithmetic mean summarizes the center of the simulated distribution and does not represent a target probability. The evaluation uses `r .fmti(NS)` realizations for each demand -- the probabilistic spectrum of each return period $T_R$ = `r paste(prettyNum(round(sort(TR.gmdp), 0), big.mark = ","), collapse = ", ")` years and the MCE -- and a grid of allowable displacements $D_a$ ranging from `r paste(format(range(Da.gmdp), trim = TRUE, scientific = FALSE), collapse = " to ")` cm. This grid contains, for every slope height, the damage thresholds $D_a/H_s$ = `r paste(.fmt(DAMAGE.CUTS, 1), collapse = ", ")`% of Chapter [-@sec-criteria], at which the coefficient tables are reported and which the coefficient figures shade; the roots of the values of $D_a$ that lie outside the calculated range of a realization follow the rules defined above.
