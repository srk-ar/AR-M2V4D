The verification of the slopes under those design earthquakes follows the practice that ICOLD and FEMA frame for dams and embankments through successive tiers of analysis: pseudostatic checks, simplified deformation estimates, and dynamic analyses when the consequence, the anticipated response, or the complexity of the mechanism requires them [@ICOLD2016; @FEMA2005]. Within this framework, the Newmark method provides a deformation-based estimate that complements the limit-equilibrium verification, and the pseudostatic coefficient of that verification is a project-specific quantity rather than a universal property of the structure type. The USACE manuals EM 1110-2-1902 and EM 1110-2-2100 include pseudostatic analysis among the methods applicable to dams and earthworks and address the sliding block for estimating permanent seismic deformation; they tie the selection of the coefficient to the site-specific hazard, the potential failure mechanism, and the performance criteria of the project, so the coefficient results from the conditions of each project instead of a single value applicable to all of them [@USACE2003; @USACE2005].

Two further references relate the pseudostatic verification to the permanent deformation. NCHRP Report 611 formulates the seismic analysis of slopes and transportation infrastructure in terms of displacement-based performance and uses simplified Newmark correlations to relate demand to permanent deformation [@AndersonEtAl2008], and Eurocode 8 ties the pseudostatic action to the design seismic acceleration and the consequence class and admits Newmark displacement calculations when the ratio between the critical acceleration and the design acceleration justifies a deformation-based verification [@CEN2004]. These references provide the framing and the comparison criteria for the coefficients of this report; their indicative values serve that comparison, and the displacement objective defined for the project governs the coefficients themselves.

That objective is expressed as an allowable permanent displacement relative to the slope height. The coefficients of this report are read against four damage thresholds of the relative permanent displacement, $D_a/H$ = `r paste(.fmt(DAMAGE.CUTS, 1), collapse = ", ")`%: the crest-settlement ratios derived from ninety case histories of earthquake damage to embankment dams and associated with the Operational, Damage, Life Safety and Collapse limit states of the Italian dam code NTD14 [@Aliberti2019]. Their denominator, the embankment height plus the thickness of the deformable foundation, is the slope height used here. The coefficient tables are reported at those thresholds and the coefficient figures shade the intervals they delimit; the derivation of the coefficients from the allowable displacement is developed in Chapter [-@sec-coefficients].
