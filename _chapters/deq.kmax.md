The verification of the slopes under those design earthquakes follows the practice that ICOLD and FEMA frame for dams and embankments through successive tiers of analysis including pseudostatic stability checks, simplified estimates of permanent deformation, and dynamic analyses where warranted by the consequences of failure, anticipated response, or complexity of the failure mechanism [@ICOLD2016; @FEMA2005]. Within this framework, the Newmark method provides a deformation-based assessment that complements the limit-equilibrium analysis. The pseudostatic seismic coefficient is a project-specific value rather than a universal value assigned to a particular structure type. USACE manuals EM 1110-2-1902 and EM 1110-2-2100 include pseudostatic analysis among the methods applicable to dams and earthworks and address the sliding block for estimating permanent seismic deformation. Selection of the coefficient considers the site-specific seismic hazard, potential failure mechanism, and project performance criteria, so the coefficient results from the conditions of each project instead of a single value applicable to all of them [@USACE2003; @USACE2005].

Two further references relate the pseudostatic verification to the permanent deformation. NCHRP Report 611 formulates the seismic analysis of slopes and transportation infrastructure in terms of displacement-based performance and uses simplified Newmark correlations to relate demand to permanent deformation [@AndersonEtAl2008], and Eurocode 8 ties the pseudostatic action to the design seismic acceleration and the consequence class and admits Newmark displacement calculations when the ratio between the critical acceleration and the design acceleration justifies a deformation-based verification [@CEN2004]. These references provide the framing and the comparison criteria for the coefficients of this report; their indicative values serve that comparison, and the displacement objective defined for the project governs the coefficients themselves.

That objective is expressed as an allowable permanent displacement relative to the slope height. This report evaluates the coefficients at four damage thresholds of the relative permanent displacement, $D_a/H_s$ = `r paste(.fmt(DAMAGE.CUTS, 1), collapse = ", ")`%: the crest-settlement ratios derived from ninety case histories of earthquake damage to embankment dams and associated with the Operational, Damage, Life Safety and Collapse limit states of the Italian dam code NTD14 [@Aliberti2019]. Their denominator, the reference height, includes the embankment height and the thickness of the deformable foundation and is adopted here as the slope height for normalization. The seismic coefficient tables are presented for these four thresholds and the coefficient figures shade the intervals they delimit. Chapter [-@sec-coefficients] describes the derivation of the coefficients from the allowable displacement.
