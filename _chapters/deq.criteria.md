The seismic design of tailings storage facilities is based on the two-level performance framework consolidated by dam-safety practice: an Operating Basis Earthquake (OBE), which the facility must withstand without operational interruption, and a Safety Evaluation Earthquake (SEE), under which it must not release tailings. ICOLD Bulletin 148 formalizes this framework for embankment structures [@ICOLD2016; @ANCOLD2012], and the USACE Engineer Regulation ER 1110-2-1806 expresses it in terms of the OBE and the Maximum Design Earthquake (MDE) [@USACE1995].

Three international frameworks implement these two performance levels for tailings storage facilities according to the consequence class of the facility: the Global Industry Standard on Tailings Management (GISTM) [@gistm2020], the Canadian Dam Association (CDA) guidelines [@cda2021; @CDA2019], and the Australian National Committee on Large Dams (ANCOLD) guidelines [@ancold2019]. The consequence class, from Low to Extreme, reflects the potential severity of a failure in terms of population at risk, anticipated loss of life, and environmental and socio-economic impacts, governs the stringency of the seismic criteria, and sets progressively more stringent annual exceedance probabilities (AEPs) for the design earthquakes. GISTM defines five consequence classes, CDA five consequence classifications, and ANCOLD six consequence categories, with three gradations, High C, High B and High A, within its High category. The classes and the design-earthquake AEPs of each framework are tabulated in Tables [-@tbl-gistm], [-@tbl-cda] and [-@tbl-ancold].

Each framework designates the design earthquake in its own terms. GISTM specifies a single design ground motion for each consequence class and lifecycle stage, with the Maximum Credible Earthquake (MCE) evaluated in parallel and governing whenever it produces the higher demand [-@gistm2020]. CDA adopts the SEE as the design basis, admits the MCE as a declared alternative to the probabilistic level for high-consequence facilities, and allows risk-based departures for lower classes during transitional closure stages provided the post-closure requirements are ultimately met; its tabulated levels are targets for initial consideration and consultation between owner and regulator, and the designer of record and the owner may adopt more stringent levels to further reduce risk, or less stringent ones where justified [-@cda2021]. ANCOLD assigns an OBE and an SEE to each consequence category: a uniform OBE of 1/500 AEP together with an SEE that ranges from 1/1,000 AEP up to the greater of the MCE and the 1/10,000 AEP event (84th percentile) for the Extreme category [-@ancold2019], in line with mining-sector regulatory guidance that assigns design return periods of 10,000 years or longer to extreme-consequence structures [@UBCThesis].

For a given consequence class, the design earthquake also varies with the lifecycle stage. During construction, partial-height embankments are checked against the OBE as a minimum, and against the SEE for high-consequence facilities or in high-seismicity regions. During operation, the stage with the largest stored volumes and the highest risk profile, the differentiation by consequence class is greatest: operational AEPs range from 1/200 (GISTM, Low) or 1/100 (CDA) to 1/10,000 for Extreme facilities. Closure and post-closure criteria are specific to each framework. GISTM retains the operational criteria through active-care closure and sets a 1/10,000 AEP for all classes during passive-care post-closure [@gistm2020]. CDA retains criteria that depend on the consequence classification through closure and passive care: closure under active care with regular monitoring retains the levels of the construction, operation and transition stages, and passive care, which is distinct from abandonment of the facility, begins only once a steady-state condition has been demonstrated by accumulated experience and monitoring; any risk-based transition must be justified [@cda2021; @CDA2019]. ANCOLD adopts for post-closure the SEE of the applicable category and requires it to be reassessed at closure when the hazard, the condition of the dam, or its consequences change [@ancold2019].

In this report, the MDE is represented by the probabilistic spectrum for a 10,000-year return period, whereas the MCE is represented by the deterministic envelope. GISTM requires comparison of the MDE and MCE for the Extreme class during operations and active-care closure, and for every class during passive-care post-closure [@gistm2020]. For CDA, the alternative between the probabilistic level and the MCE is set against the MDE for the Extreme class during construction, operation, and transition, and for the Very High and Extreme classes during closure and passive care [@cda2021]. ANCOLD assigns the MDE to the High A category and, for the Extreme category, requires the greater of the MDE and MCE [@ancold2019].

Design ground motions for slopes are reported by lifecycle stage: operation, closure, and post-closure. The design earthquake that applies to each stage, whether the OBE, the SEE, or the PCE, is the one that each standard assigns to the consequence class of the facility in Tables [-@tbl-gistm], [-@tbl-cda], and [-@tbl-ancold]: ANCOLD designates it as the OBE or the SEE of the consequence category, GISTM as the design ground motion of the class and stage, and CDA as the Earthquake Design Ground Motion (EDGM) of the consequence classification. For each site condition, the probabilistic demand of a criterion is derived by linking its prescribed return period to the available uniform hazard spectrum (UHS); where the criterion is defined between two return periods, the design demand is the average of the demands evaluated at both, and the UHS is taken at its mean, except for the ANCOLD Extreme-category closure criterion, which is taken at the 84th percentile. Where the Extreme-class criterion invokes the MCE, the MCE (mean) and MCE (84%) series accompany the design spectra. The design PGA and design spectra are reported by framework, consequence class, lifecycle stage, and site condition.
