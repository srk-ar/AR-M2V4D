- **Newmark Permanent Displacements.** The Newmark permanent displacement $D_n$ quantifies the displacement induced by an earthquake and accumulated in the sliding mass and is the performance measure from which the seismic coefficients are derived. The $D_n(k_y)$ curves relate, for each combination of geometry, material, and design scenario, the yield acceleration $k_y$ to the expected permanent displacement; they were computed with the `ensemble` set of `AM88`, `JB07`, `SR08`, `BT07`, and `BM19`, with the reference magnitude assigned to each seismic demand and 300 realizations per demand and are read at the fractiles $q = 1 - p_e$.

The Newmark displacements were computed with the `ensemble` set of five formulations: `AM88`, `JB07`, and `SR08` for the rigid block, and `BT07` and `BM19` for the flexible block, `BM19` being the branch for projects with crustal control, with equal weights. Every formulation received the reference magnitude assigned to each seismic demand and the demand of each of the 8 return periods, 100, 200, 500, 1,000, 2,000, 2,500, 5,000, and 10,000 years, and of the MCE, with 300 realizations per demand, over the 5 slope heights from 27 to 135 m, the 8 material classes, and the relative displacement grid $D_a/H_s$ = 0.05, 0.10, 0.25, 0.40, 0.50, 1.00, and 2.50 %.
